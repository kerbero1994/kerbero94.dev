What SITIMM taught me

A year as the only engineer on a platform for 70,000 union members. These changed how I build.

  • Meet people where they already publish.

    The comms team already posted every notice to a WhatsApp channel, so the CMS was a second, manual job. Now a bot reads the channel, AI drafts the post in 8 languages, and an admin approves it from email or WhatsApp.

  • Nobody wants a ticket. They want an answer.

    Members didn't get how a ticket queue works; they wanted results right away. Consultations became a conversation, and SitimmVito, the app's assistant, answers from the union's own documents in seconds.

  • Approving should take one click.

    A new magazine used to wait for approval before its page and promo video were even made. Now everything is built the moment the issue is detected; approving only makes it official.

  • Show people it saved.

    Autosave felt modern to me. The admins, who aren't technical, didn't trust it. A clear “Save changes” button, visible states and a success message fixed that.

  • “Sent” is not “delivered”.

    The server counted every notification as sent. When I measured, almost no phones were subscribed. Now each notice shows how many people could get it, received it and opened it.

  • Unused isn't unneeded.

    I proposed removing features with zero usage. The client stopped me: the panel was new, so zero measured adoption, not need. Broken things get fixed, not deleted.
